Guide

How SignSense understands a sign

Your webcam pictures are never classified directly. Each frame becomes 21 hand points, and only those numbers are compared with what the model learned from your own recordings.

💬 Text + 🔊 Speech
1

Record your signs

Run python collect_data.py. Hold each sign and move your hand slightly while 200 samples are recorded. Only landmark numbers are saved.

2

Train the model

Run python train_model.py. It compares three classifiers, keeps the best one, and reports its accuracy on samples it never trained on.

3

Recognise live

Press Start camera. The sign appears as text with a confidence bar, and a steady sign is spoken once.

4

Practise with the quiz

Choose Practice. Hold the target sign for about a second. An exact match scores a point; 12 seconds of nothing counts as a miss.

Tips for better accuracy

  • Use the same hand for recording and recognising.
  • Good front light and a plain background.
  • Keep your whole hand in view, about an arm's length away.
  • Record at slightly different distances and angles.
  • Make sure no two signs look almost identical.
  • Hold the sign still for about a second.

What it cannot do

  • No continuous or sentence-level translation.
  • Reads one hand as a still pose; movement and two-handed signs are not captured.
  • Ignores face and body expression.
  • Only knows the signs listed in Vocabulary.
  • One person and one webcam at a time.
📘

Check your handshapes. SignSense does not ship an ISL dictionary. Learn the correct sign for each letter, number and word from an official source such as the ISLRTC Indian Sign Language dictionary (islrtc.nic.in) before recording, so you train the model on real ISL.
